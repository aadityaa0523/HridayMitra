HridayMitra: requirements, parts list and complete build guide

Monitoring only, not a diagnosis tool. Never test on a person with a pacemaker or defibrillator. Circuit values are starting values: check them in LTspice before you build.

What changed in v3 (cost trim, team-approved)
  • Product parts list is now ₹4,695 at safe prices (was ₹7,095), about ₹305 under the ₹5,000 limit.
  • One HX711 for all four load cells. The 4 half-bridge cells are wired as one full bridge, the same way a bathroom scale is wired. Total weight and stand-up speed are unchanged; we lose the per-corner readings. This frees GPIO26, 27 and 33.
  • Voice from the ESP32's own flash memory. The DFPlayer and microSD card are gone. Prompts are stored in flash and played through the second DAC (GPIO26) into a PAM8403 amplifier and the same 4 Ω 3 W speaker.
  • No boot pins used any more. The path-select lines moved from GPIO12 and GPIO0 to the freed GPIO33 and GPIO27.
  • Trimmed prices: resistors and capacitors are counted as used, panel buttons replace arcade buttons, one 18650 cell, one perfboard, and a plain stool.
What changed in v2
  • Lung fluid is now measured physically. The 4 hand strips are wired as electrodes. Two 74HC4052 switches pick 4 current paths (legs, arms, right side, left side), and the ESP32 computes a trunk (chest) impedance. This replaces the simulation-only chest belt. New rows and wiring are marked in yellow.
  • Prices revised from Indian retail listings (Probots, Robocraze, Hubtronics, Robu, Thingbits, Zbotic, IndiaMART), searched on 24 Oct 2026. ✓ = found online; the rest are estimates.
  • The ESP32 is already bought. It costs nothing more to buy, but its market price (₹499) is counted in the product parts list.
  • Instrumentation amplifier fixed. The INA128 and AD620 need at least about 4.5 V, so they won't run on our single 3.3 V supply. Use the INA126 (SOIC, on an adapter) or an INA333 breakout. The fallback is a 3-op-amp version built from 2 × MCP6002.
  • Battery, voice and button prices went up; the totals are recomputed in section 3.
Changed after the simulations (24 Oct 2026): fit these on the build
  • R8 and R9 are now 10 MΩ, not 1 MΩ. An LTspice Monte Carlo of 100 stools built from 5% parts, on random bodies and skin contacts, found that with 1 MΩ, 1 in 100 stools read more than 7.6% wrong (worst 12%) and the chest value was unusable. Cause: a dry return foot moves the whole body's voltage, and unequal sense contacts against 1 MΩ turn that into a false difference. With 10 MΩ, 99% of readings are within 2%, a +10% fluid change is measured within 0.13 points, and the amplifier never clips (input bias current included). The price is the same. Files: sim/circuit/montecarlo.py, HOW_TO_VERIFY.md.
  • Mains hum (simulated): 230 V / 50 Hz coupled into the body and sense wires, on battery and with a phone charger plugged in. The 5 kHz Goertzel reading stayed within 0.17% in every case, while a plain peak-to-peak reading was up to 18% wrong with the charger plugged in. So: the firmware must use the Goertzel amplitude, never peak-to-peak, and measure on battery with the charger unplugged (this is also the safety rule). Use short sense wires (shielded cable from the plates if longer than about 30 cm). File: sim/circuit/hum.py.
  • Toe rings: a metal ring bridging a foot's two plates reads 58–86% too high. The alert engine now rejects any leg reading more than 25% from last week's normal and the stool asks the patient to place their feet again. Keep the plate gap at least 8 cm (build pack).

5. The impedance front end (the custom circuit): every value

Idea: a 5 kHz sine goes through the body via two outer electrodes, and two inner electrodes measure the voltage. A small resistor (R5) in the current path measures the current. The ESP32 reads both through the same amplifier, so Z = R5 × (voltage across the body ÷ voltage across R5) and the gain cancels. The bias point (Vref) is 1.65 V, half of 3.3 V. New in v2: two 74HC4052 switches choose which electrodes carry current and which sense, so the same circuit measures four body paths.

The four paths (S1 S0 on GPIO27, GPIO33)

S1 S0PathCurrent in → outSense +, −Measures
0 0Z1 legsLF_FORE → RF_FORELF_HEEL, RF_HEELleft leg + right leg (leg fluid)
0 1Z2 armsLH_OUT → RH_OUTLH_IN, RH_INleft arm + right arm
1 0Z3 right sideRH_OUT → RF_FORERH_IN, RF_HEELright arm + trunk + right leg

Trunk (lung-fluid proxy): Ztrunk = (Z3 + Z4 − Z1 − Z2) ÷ 2. Fluid in the chest lowers Ztrunk. This is the segment model used by 8-electrode body-composition scales. It is untested for heart failure. The trunk is only about 20–50 Ω, while each path is a few hundred ohms, so the subtraction magnifies noise: trend it over days, never one reading.

Check pin numbers on the datasheets (INA126, MCP6022/MCP6002, 74HC4053, 74HC4052) before wiring. The 74HC4052 switch resistance (about 100 Ω at 3.3 V) sits in the current loop; it doesn't affect the result, because the current is measured across R5. All switched signals stay between 0 and 3.3 V because they are centred on VREF.

Expected levels (estimates): current about 50–100 µA peak. Across the legs about 35–70 mV peak, so 0.35–0.7 V after the amplifier. Across R5 about 11–22 mV, so 110–220 mV after the amplifier. If the R5 reading is too small, raise R5. If the body reading clips, lower the excitation scale.

Timing: 4 paths × 2 select states × about 100 ms, plus 0.2 s settling after each path change, × 3 repeats (median) = about 4–5 s, inside the 60 s sit.

Resistor-phantom test (do this before any person touches it)

  1. Legs only: put 330 Ω between LF_FORE and RF_FORE, with LF_HEEL on the LF_FORE end and RF_HEEL on the RF_FORE end. Path Z1 should read 330 Ω.
  2. Repeat with 680 Ω and 1 kΩ. The computed Z should follow the resistor within about 5%. If it doesn't, check the select lines, the bias resistors and the ADC sampling.
  3. Add 1–5 kΩ in series with each outer lead to mimic poor contact. Z should barely move, because the current is measured.
  4. Whole-body phantom (new). Build a star of five resistors:
    • left arm 330 Ω from the left-hand node to a "chest" node
    • right arm 330 Ω from the right-hand node to the chest node
    • trunk 47 Ω from the chest node to a "pelvis" node
    • left leg 330 Ω from the pelvis to the left-foot node
    • right leg 330 Ω from the pelvis to the right-foot node
    Clip each hand's OUT and IN to its hand node, and each foot's FORE and HEEL to its foot node. Expect Z1 ≈ 660, Z2 ≈ 660, Z3 ≈ Z4 ≈ 707, so Ztrunk ≈ 47 Ω. Then change the trunk to 33 Ω ("more chest fluid") and check that the computed trunk drops by about 14 Ω. Report the error.

Digital measurement: sample GPIO35 at 40 kHz or more (ADC continuous mode). Take a Goertzel (a fast single-frequency filter) at 5 kHz over about 100 ms (a whole number of 20 ms mains cycles) for each select state, and compute the amplitude ratio. Never use peak-to-peak: mains hum makes it up to 18% wrong when a charger is plugged in (simulated). If the ADC is too slow, drop the excitation to 2 kHz. Let it settle for about 1 s after switching the DAC on. Use the median of three readings.

LTspice proof (graph for the judges): model each segment (arm, trunk, leg) as outside-fluid resistance in parallel with inside-fluid resistance plus membrane capacitance, and add 1–10 kΩ of contact resistance on each electrode. Sweep leg fluid ±10% and trunk fluid ±10% separately, at 5 kHz and 50 kHz. Show that leg fluid moves Z1, trunk fluid moves Ztrunk, and contact resistance barely moves either. Report the error.

7. Firmware and data

Modules: sensors (weight, impedance on 4 paths, pulse), quality checks (stillness, contact, signal), identity check (weight and impedance fit the patient), serial output, LED and button handler, and voice prompts played from flash (DAC2), plus optional SMS.

Daily features file (the contract between hardware, data and engine): patient_id, day, weight_kg, leg_z_ohm, leg_z_evening_ohm, arm_z_ohm, trunk_z_ohm, pulse_bpm, spo2, standup_s, breathless, orthopnea, took_pills, amulet_steps, amulet_situps, quality. A missing value is an empty cell, never 0.

Quality rules:

  • Reject weight unless the load is steady for 5 s.
  • Reject an impedance path if its select-high reading is below a floor (no current flowing).
  • Reject the trunk value if any of Z1–Z4 failed, or if a hand lost grip (Z2 jumps above 3× its baseline).
  • Reject pulse with a low quality score.
Show "no reading" instead of guessing.

Tare: zero the empty stool on every power-up. Re-check with a 5 kg known weight weekly.

8. Amulet prototype (if time)

  1. Wire the MPU6050 to the S3 Sense: 3.3 V, GND, SDA, SCL. Read acceleration at 25–50 Hz.
  2. Compute the tilt angle from gravity: lying flat, propped, upright. Count a "sit-up" as upright for more than 5 minutes between midnight and 5 am (shorten the time for the demo).
  3. Count steps and movement minutes; flag worn or not worn from tiny movements.
  4. Send a Bluetooth notification with angle, steps, sit-up flag and the SOS press.
  5. Test angles with a protractor jig (within 5 degrees). Test breathing only with the servo breathing dummy (target: within 2 breaths a minute in at least 80% of windows), and report it as experimental.

Pendant vs night-clip mounting is still an open team decision. A free-hanging pendant swings and slides, which weakens the posture and breathing readings.

9. Safety and edge-case checklist

  • Battery power whenever a person touches the electrodes; never mains, and unplug the charger during a check-in (the firmware can refuse to measure while the TP4056 reports charging). Keep the current near 0.1 mA at 5 kHz and confirm against IEC 60601-1 before any real use.
  • The arms and side paths send current across the chest. Keep the ~0.1 mA limit in firmware (cap the DAC amplitude), and stop if the R5 reading shows more than 0.15 mA. 8-electrode body-composition scales work the same way, but our circuit is unverified.
  • No pacemaker or defibrillator users. No magnets anywhere. All impedance off for implant patients.
  • Round and insulate all metal edges. Keep the 18650 away from heat and metal; use a protected cell.
  • Touch a grounded metal object before handling the ICs (static).
  • Test with dry feet and hands only at first. Test wet feet after the circuit is verified.
