Early warning of worsening heart failure: who builds what

A wrist band records the patient, a bedside dock decides, and an SMS reaches the family's phone. Five jobs that run in parallel: everyone builds against the fixed file formats on this page and uses the sample rows as mock input. You only join the pieces at the final merge.

Hour 0: decide together (30 min)

The only shared step. Confirm the architecture below, write a name next to each role, and paste the contracts into CONTRACT.md in the repo. After that, nobody changes a contract without telling all five.

Why the dock decides

The alert engine runs on the dock, not in the cloud. Power cuts and no internet don't stop it, and SMS works on a basic 2G/4G signal.

The twist judges won't see elsewhere

Lying-down response. When the band detects the patient lying down at night, it compares breathing rate and heart rate in the first 10 minutes lying flat against the minutes just before. A growing rise points to trouble breathing when lying flat, picked up passively.

Patient effort

Wear it. Put it on the dock during the morning bath (about 30 min charge). Press the button once if breathless when it buzzes. Under 1 minute a day.

Gaps to close or admit

No weight. A band can't see fluid build-up, which is the best-evidenced signal (Chaudhry 2007). Cheapest fix: make the dock's base a load-cell plate the patient stands on while putting the band back on (+₹1,100). Otherwise say so in "limits".
Wear and charging. Elderly patients take bands off. Count wear hours every day, treat low wear as "no data" (not "normal"), and SMS the family after 2 days with no wear.
Wrist PPG is noisy. It is worse with movement and darker skin. Only use readings from still periods (night, rest), and leave wrist SpO2 out of the alert score.
SMS limits in India. Tamil or Hindi SMS fits only 70 characters per message (English: 160). SIM800L is 2G-only and Jio has no 2G; an A7670C 4G module is safer (about ₹1,300). Sending SMS from a SIM is fine for a prototype; a real service would need TRAI DLT registration.
Ambulance auto-call (later). With several false alerts per patient-month, never dispatch automatically. Escalate: SMS family → SMS doctor → call only after a human confirms or nobody responds.

4 · How results are scored (P4)

  • Caught: a red alert on any day from admit_day − 14 to admit_day − 1.
  • Lead time: admit_day minus the first red day in that window. Report the median.
  • Normal days: every day of stable patients, plus days before worsen_start_day for patients with an event. Specificity is the share of these that are not red.
  • False alerts: red days on normal days, counted as new alerts (consecutive red days count once), per patient per 30 days.
  • Split: 70% of patients for tuning, 30% for testing, seed 42. Report numbers on the test patients only.

5 · Alerts: alerts.json (P4 writes, P5 reads)

Final merge (all five, last ~3 hours)

  • P3's data goes into P4's evaluate.py, and the real numbers go into results.md.
  • P4's alerts.json and SMS texts go into P5's phone demo and web view.
  • The report gets P4's metrics, P2's accuracy numbers, P1's parts total, battery estimate and simulation screenshots, and P3's DATA.md summary.
  • Rehearse the 5-minute pitch twice.

This merge is the only point where anyone needs someone else's work. If someone falls behind, the others can still demo with the mock files.
