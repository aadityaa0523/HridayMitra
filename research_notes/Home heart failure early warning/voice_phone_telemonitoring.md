# Voice/Speech, Smartphone and Symptom Check-ins, and Large Telemonitoring Trials for Detecting Worsening HF at Home

Note on method: research done Oct 2026 with web search plus selected page fetches. NEJM, Lancet and PubMed pages blocked automated fetching (403 or cookie wall), so some trial numbers come from secondary news or journal-summary pages (tctmd, MDedge/The Hospitalist, ACC.org, Healio). These are flagged where relevant.

## 1. Speech/voice analysis (Cordio HearO, Amir et al., counting/breath tests, cough, language independence)

### Takeaway
Cordio HearO has the most data of any voice tool. Patients record a few fixed sentences daily on a smartphone. Across company-linked studies from 2021 to 2025 it reports 71-85% sensitivity for HF events, about 24-26 days of warning, and about 2.4-3 false alerts per patient-year, with 83.5% daily-recording adherence over about 4 years. All of this is retrospective or observational, comes from company-affiliated investigators, and has no outcome RCT. DETECT-HF, the pivotal prospective blinded study, had not been located in published form at the time of search. Counting and breath-hold tests such as the Roth score have weak validation.

### Cited Findings
**Amir et al.: wet vs dry proof of concept (Cordio-affiliated authors; app is proprietary HearO)**
- JACC: Heart Failure 2022. 40 patients hospitalized with acute decompensated HF recorded 5 fixed sentences on a smartphone at admission ("wet") and at discharge ("dry"). Discharge recordings were tagged as distinct from baseline in 94% of cases. The largest change was in speech measure SM2 (218%) — [tctmd](https://www.tctmd.com/news/smartphone-app-hears-speech-changes-indicating-fluid-buildup-hf); [EuroIntervention summary](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/changes-in-voice-patterns-for-monitoring-heart-failure-deterioration)
- In 1,728 recordings from 47 patients, all 5 speech measures differed between wet and dry states (p<0.0001), with changes from baseline of 49-200% — [EuroIntervention summary](https://eurointervention.pcronline.com/article/changes-in-voice-patterns-for-monitoring-heart-failure-deterioration)
- ESC Heart Failure 2021 hemodialysis feasibility study: chronic HF patients on scheduled dialysis, used as a model of fluid removal. In a 5-patient subset, speech measures rose about 23% from pre-dialysis by the end of dialysis and stayed there until the next morning — [DOAJ / ESC Heart Failure Aug 2021](https://doaj.org/article/fa84d52ff90f4f9ea36560cc005d7b07)

**Cordio HearO in the community (presented by W.T. Abraham, who discloses Cordio consulting fees)**
- ESC-HF 2022 preliminary study: 180 stable HF patients. The system predicted 32 of 39 first HF events (82% sensitivity), with an 18% false-positive rate and an estimated 2-3 false alarms per patient per year — [MDedge](https://mdedge.com/ecardiologynews/article/254876/heart-failure/voice-analysis-app-promising-early-warning-system-heart)
- HearO Community Study (AHA 2023), training phase: 44 of 58 HF events detected (76%) and 81% of first events (n=35), on average 24 days before hospitalization or need for IV diuretics. Validation phase: 10 of 14 events (71%) and 77% of first events, on average 26 days ahead. About 3 unnecessary alerts per patient-year in both phases — [MDedge/The Hospitalist](https://mdedge.com/content/smartphone-app-detects-voice-quality-changes-indicating-worsening-heart-failure)
- COMMUNITY-HF (HFSA 2025):
  - Cohort: 599 stable HF patients (HFrEF and HFpEF) at 5 US and 10 Israeli sites, mean age 69, 71% male, mean LVEF 39.9%, mean NYHA 2.4. Follow-up was 51 months (March 2018 to March 2025), 360,420 patient-days.
  - Events and detection: 89 HF events (69 first events). Sensitivity 85.4% (14.6% false negatives). Average lead time 24.9 days, from retrospective analysis of recordings in the 31 days before each event.
  - Burden: 2.4 unexplained priority notifications (false alerts) per year. Daily-recording adherence 83.5%. More than 90% of survey respondents reported no difficulty or stress with daily recordings.
  - Critique: the independent commentator (Santos-Gallego) wants a larger trial with longer follow-up before using the app clinically.
  - Source: [tctmd](https://www.tctmd.com/news/voice-analysis-picks-patients-risk-hf-hospitalization-community-hf)
- Language independence (company claim): COMMUNITY-HF patients spoke Hebrew (about half), Russian 18.4%, Spanish 15.7%, English 9.3% and Arabic 5.8%. Authors say language did not affect performance because each patient serves as their own baseline. Language-stratified accuracy was not reported in the coverage — [tctmd](https://www.tctmd.com/news/voice-analysis-picks-patients-risk-hf-hospitalization-community-hf)
- DETECT-HF (NCT06378632, sponsor Cordio): international, multicenter, observational, prospective, blinded, single-arm, two-period study in symptomatic chronic HF with recent decompensation. It is designed for FDA approval and enrolls more English speakers with a range of accents. Results were slated for AHA November 2025 — [CenterWatch listing](https://www.centerwatch.com/clinical-trials/listings/NCT06378632/an-international-observational-blinded-study-to-assess-the-performance-of-the-cordio-hearo-system); [tctmd](https://www.tctmd.com/news/voice-analysis-picks-patients-risk-hf-hospitalization-community-hf)
- Other voice efforts:
  - Noah Labs runs a separate study, PRE-DETECT-HF (NCT07443969, pre-symptomatic voice detection). Do not confuse it with DETECT-HF — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07443969)
  - The Baker Institute (Australia) is testing AI on 1-minute weekly voice recordings for up to 52 weeks to assess breathlessness in HF — [Baker Institute](https://baker.edu.au/research/clinical-trials/ai-breathlessness-study)

**Counting / breath tests**
- Roth score (Chorin et al., Clin Cardiol 2016): the patient counts from 1 to 30 in one breath, as fast as possible, in their native language.
  - Correlation with SpO2: r=0.59 for counting time and r=0.67 for maximal count.
  - Counting time >8 s had 78% sensitivity and 73% specificity for SpO2 <95%. CEBM reports 71% specificity, a discrepancy between sources.
  - Population: 93 patients in an Israeli coronary care unit plus 103 healthy controls.
  - Sources: [PMC6490799](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6490799); [CEBM appraisal](https://cebm.net/?p=15546)
- The Oxford COVID-19 Evidence Service said that using the Roth score over the phone does not accurately assess hypoxia and may give false reassurance — [CEBM](https://cebm.net/?p=15546)
- Patent WO2014037843A1 describes a smartphone test of sustained vocal-exhalation duration as a proxy for lung capacity in HF, COPD and asthma. This is a patent, not a validation — [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2014037843A1/en)

### Inferences
- About 2.4-3 false alerts per patient-year means that a program of 1,000 patients would triage roughly 200-250 false alerts a month. That is manageable, and much lower than typical weight-rule burdens (see section 4), but it still needs a response workflow.
- Voice works as a low-effort check: about 30 seconds a day, and 83.5% adherence over 4 years is high compared with Tele-HF and BEAT-HF. However, the cohort was stable, consented, mostly Israeli patients, so selection bias is likely.
- Sensitivity was calculated retrospectively over a 31-day window. Real-time prospective performance with clinician action is untested.

### Gaps
- The DETECT-HF primary results (AHA 2025) were not found. Whether it met its prespecified sensitivity and false-alert endpoints is unknown, as is FDA status as of 2026.
- No RCT shows that voice-guided intervention reduces hospitalizations.
- No independent (non-Cordio) replication was found. Peer-reviewed full papers for the COMMUNITY-HF numbers were not located; these are conference data.
- I did not search for or find peer-reviewed data on cough detection specifically for HF decompensation.
- No accuracy data broken down by language, accent, or comorbidity (e.g., COPD, colds, dentures) was found.

## 2. Smartphone camera PPG, phone photos of ankle edema, microphone breathing rate (accuracy in elderly users)

### Takeaway
Fingertip camera PPG for AF has high pooled accuracy (about 94% sensitivity and 97% specificity) but the studies are of poor quality. In screening-age populations most positives are false (PPV about 20-40%). Edema imaging and microphone respiratory rate for HF are early-stage and mostly industry-led, with no validated outcome data found.

### Cited Findings
- O'Sullivan et al., JAMA Netw Open 2020: 10 studies, 3,852 participants. Smartphone camera AF apps had high sensitivity and specificity. However, the modeled PPV for screening people aged 65 and older with hypertension was only about 20-40%, while NPV was about 100% — [PMC7125433](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7125433/)
- Gill et al., Heart 2022:
  - 28 studies, about 11,400 participants. The pooled analysis (17 studies, 6,891 participants) gave 94% sensitivity and 97% specificity, with substantial heterogeneity.
  - The studies were judged poor quality, with selection and publication bias, and the authors called for studies independent of manufacturers.
  - Sources: [Heart](https://heart.bmj.com/content/108/20/1600); [Birmingham repository](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/smartphone-detection-of-atrial-fibrillation-using-photoplethysmog)
- Edema imaging:
  - Heartfelt Technologies (industry) uses an infrared 3D camera that scans bare feet. The company claims peripheral edema is detectable about 2 weeks before readmission, and an NHS trial is evaluating the device — [GlobalSpec](https://insights.globalspec.com/article/22237/); [trial listing](https://www.trialx.com/clinical-trials/listings/330891/monitoring-oedema-in-heart/)
  - A BCS 2022 abstract described a camera plus AI for home lower-leg volume that "does not require patient adherence" — [UCLan repository](https://clok.uclan.ac.uk/43736)
  - A 2024 conference paper describes a MobileNetV3 phone app for detecting the presence and severity of edema. No accuracy data was retrieved — [EUDL](https://eudl.eu/doi/10.1007/978-3-031-59717-6_18)

### Inferences
- For an HF early-warning product, camera PPG is credible for spot heart rate and AF rhythm checks, with AF being a common trigger of decompensation. A positive result still needs ECG confirmation, and false positives will add triage load in older users.
- Passive or near-passive edema capture addresses adherence decay, but it has no validated sensitivity or lead-time data.

### Gaps
- No age-stratified (≥75 y) PPG accuracy or failure-to-acquire rates were found. Tremor, cold fingers and poor perfusion are plausible failure modes, but no sourced figure was found.
- No peer-reviewed accuracy data was found for phone-photo pitting edema or for microphone-derived breathing rate in HF.
- PPG-derived HRV as a decompensation predictor was not found.

## 3. Symptom questionnaires via app/IVR/SMS (KCCQ, daily symptoms, orthopnea/pillow count, bendopnea)

### Takeaway
I found no peer-reviewed study showing that frequent (daily or weekly) app-collected KCCQ or symptom-score declines predict HF hospitalization with reported sensitivity or lead time. Bendopnea is the best-validated single bedside symptom question. Pillow count is a clinical convention with weak formal validation.

### Cited Findings
- A 2025 multicenter prospective cohort of hospitalized HF patients found bendopnea independently predicted adverse events over 1.5 years:
  - HF rehospitalization 35.1% vs 22.3%; all-cause death 19.7% vs 12.4%; arrhythmias requiring intervention 20.7% vs 11.7%.
  - Adjusted HR 1.6 (95% CI 1.3-2.0).
  - Bendopnea has been linked to elevated RA pressure and PCWP.
  - Source: [Frontiers Cardiovasc Med 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fcvm.2025.1659830/full)
- An ESC-HF 2026 abstract on people at high risk of HF found bendopnea in 31.5% (158 participants), assessed against IVC, jugular vein and lung-ultrasound congestion criteria. The association results were not retrieved — [CiteDrive](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/bendopnoea-in-patients-at-high-risk-of-heart-failure-prevalence-and-association-with-markers-of-cardiac-function-and-congestion/)
- Pillow count is confounded by habitual pillow use, so two pillows should not be assumed to mean orthopnea. This source is a pharmacy education blog, not a validation study — [UMaryland Pharmacy blog](https://blogs.pharmacy.umaryland.edu/atrium/?p=424)
- Small app trials:
  - A 28-patient app study (median 60 days) found that duration of app use correlated with KCCQ gains — [PMC7857947](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7857947)
  - HF-SMART (n=30) found no difference in 30-day readmission: 18.8% vs 13.3% — [PMC8080140 table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8080140/table/table1)
- ePRO-HF collected weekly 1-10 symptom scales plus monthly KCCQ-12 for ≥6 months. Predictive results were not reported in what was retrieved — [ICHOM abstract](https://conference.ichom.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/55-Abstract.pdf)
- In the TwoCan Pulse telemonitoring cohort (n=111, JMIR Cardio 2024), phone-reported congestive symptoms were one of four alert triggers. The other triggers were SpO2 <90%, HR >110 and weight gain ≥2 kg — [JMIR Cardio 2024](https://cardio.jmir.org/2024/1/e52648)

### Inferences
- Symptom questions work best as a confirmatory layer on top of a physiological or voice signal, rather than as a stand-alone early warning. Patients often notice symptoms late, which is the rationale for voice and hemodynamic monitoring.
- A structured orthopnea question ("can you lie flat?") or bendopnea question is cheap to add to an IVR, SMS or voice flow. No home-validated cut-offs were found, though.

### Gaps
- No sensitivity or specificity was found for daily IVR or SMS symptom checklists versus HF events.
- Remote KCCQ-12 decline thresholds that predict events were not found. Known literature on serial KCCQ change from clinic data could not be verified here.

## 4. Large telemonitoring RCTs: signals, adherence over time, alert handling, why they succeeded or failed

### Takeaway
TIM-HF2 succeeded, reducing mortality by about 30%. It combined daily multi-parameter transmission (weight, blood pressure, ECG, SpO2, self-rated health) with a 24/7 physician-staffed telemedical centre that acted on data, in a selected recently-hospitalized NYHA II-III cohort. Trials that collected data with weak response loops or poor adherence were neutral: Tele-HF (IVR, only 55% still using it ≥3 times/week by the end) and BEAT-HF (only 55% adherent to telemonitoring at 30 days). TEN-HMS showed that the structured contact itself, nurse telephone or telemonitoring, cut mortality versus usual care, while telemonitoring added little over nurse calls.

### Cited Findings
**TIM-HF2 (Koehler et al., Lancet 2018; positive)**
- Population and follow-up: 1,538 patients enrolled 2013-2018, with 12-month follow-up — [ESC365](https://esc365.escardio.org/journal/53058)
- What was transmitted: daily weight, blood pressure, ECG and self-rated health status, under HF nurse guidance — [ESC365](https://esc365.escardio.org/journal/53058)
- Response loop: daily vitals sent to a telemedical centre, plus patient education and direct communication between the centre, cardiologist and GP — [The Hospitalist](https://blogs.the-hospitalist.org/content/home-telemonitoring-heart-failure-cuts-mortality)
- All-cause mortality: 7.86 vs 11.34 per 100 person-years, about 30% relative reduction — [The Hospitalist](https://blogs.the-hospitalist.org/content/home-telemonitoring-heart-failure-cuts-mortality); [Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/cardiology/20180828/remote-patient-management-improves-hf-outcomes-timhf2)
- Days lost to unplanned cardiovascular hospitalization or death: 17.8 vs 24.2 days per year — [The Hospitalist](https://blogs.the-hospitalist.org/content/home-telemonitoring-heart-failure-cuts-mortality)
- The authors speculated that centre-triggered early admissions prevented longer stays — [Reliasmedia](https://reliasmedia.com/articles/143472-telemedicine-improves-outcomes-in-heart-failure)
- A post hoc adherence and patient-experience analysis exists (Eur J Cardiovasc Nurs 2022), but its adherence percentages were not retrieved — [ESC365](https://esc365.escardio.org/journal/53058)
- The predecessor TIM-HF (710 patients, neutral) had more than 80% adherence: absolute 81.8 ± 22.8%, relative 88.9 ± 21.5% (n=354 telemonitored) — [Thieme abstract](https://thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/abstract/10.1055/s-0034-1369849)

**Tele-HF (Chaudhry et al., NEJM 2010; neutral)**
- 1,653 recently hospitalized HF patients were randomized to telemonitoring or usual care. Only 55% were still using the system at least 3 times a week by the end of the trial — [NEJM CardioExchange Q&A with Chaudhry](https://blogs.nejm.org/cardioexchange/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/4565)

**BEAT-HF (Ong et al., JAMA Intern Med 2016; neutral)**
- Design: 1,437 hospitalized HF patients (715 intervention vs 722 usual care), 180-day follow-up. The intervention was pre-discharge education plus nurse telephone coaching and home telemonitoring of weight, BP, HR and symptoms.
- Adherence at 30 days: 61% to the telephone calls and 55% to telemonitoring.
- Outcome: 180-day readmission about 50% in both arms; mortality similar.
- A correction to the published Results text and a table was later issued; the affected numbers were not verified.
- Sources: [PMC4827701](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4827701); [AAFP summary](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2016/0715/p168a.html); [iMedicalApps](https://www.imedicalapps.com/2016/02/heart-failure-remote-monitoring-beat-hf/)

**TEN-HMS (Cleland et al., JACC 2005)**
- Design: 426-427 patients with recent HF admission and LVEF <40%, randomized 2:2:1 to home telemonitoring, nurse telephone support or usual care.
- Primary endpoint, % days lost to death or hospitalization at 240 days: 12.7% for telemonitoring vs 15.9% for nurse support, not significant.
- Mortality at about 200 days: 16% usual care vs 8% nurse support vs 8% telemonitoring (p=0.03).
- Telemonitoring had more admissions, but they were shorter.
- Source: [ACC.org trial summary](https://www.acc.org/Latest-in-Cardiology/Clinical-Trials/2013/05/30/16/25/TENHMS)

**OSICAT (Galinier et al., Eur J Heart Fail 2020;22:985-94; neutral on primary)**
- Daily weight on connected scales plus device-reported symptoms.
- The group's own later paper lists OSICAT with Tele-HF, TIM-HF and BEAT-HF as not reducing mortality, unlike TIM-HF2. The same paper says the most benefit has been seen in NYHA III-IV and compliant patients, and that depression compromises benefit.
- An 18-month cost analysis found costs 2% lower with telemonitoring, mainly from 21% lower nurse fees.
- Source: [Frontiers Cardiovasc Med 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cardiovascular-medicine/articles/10.3389/fcvm.2022.943778/pdf)

**Alert quality (weight-rule false alerts)**
- TwoCan Pulse cohort (n=111):
  - 39 patients (35.1%) hit an HF alert and 28 (25%) had confirmed HF destabilization. No decompensation was missed.
  - There were 11 incorrect alerts: 45% from inaccurate measurement, 27% from supraventricular arrhythmia, plus infections.
  - Weight gain ≥2 kg in 4 days was associated with correct alerts (p=0.004). HR >110 was associated with incorrect alerts (p=0.007).
  - End-digit preference in self-entered values produces false alerts and alert fatigue.
  - Source: [JMIR Cardio 2024](https://cardio.jmir.org/2024/1/e52648)

### Inferences
- The pattern across trials is that outcomes depend on the response loop, not the sensor. TIM-HF2 had a physician-staffed centre able to change therapy, and TEN-HMS showed structured human contact drove the mortality benefit. Tele-HF and BEAT-HF paired data collection with weak triage or action pathways and lost about half of patients' engagement within 1-6 months.
- Adherence decays fastest in the first month after discharge. BEAT-HF dropped to 55% by day 30, and Tele-HF reached about 55% by the end. Voice-based HearO reported 83.5% over 4 years, which suggests very low-effort tasks hold up better. These cohorts differ, however: post-discharge RCT vs stable volunteers.
- Selection matters. TIM-HF2 required a recent HF hospitalization and enrolled compliant NYHA II-III patients. Benefit is concentrated in sicker, adherent, non-depressed patients.

### Gaps
- No month-by-month adherence decay curves were retrieved for any trial. Tele-HF's frequently cited "14% never used the system" figure could not be verified from the primary source here (NEJM/PubMed blocked). TIM-HF2 adherence percentages from the Lancet appendix and the 2022 nursing paper were not retrieved.
- Alert volumes per patient-year in TIM-HF2, Tele-HF and BEAT-HF were not found.
- Primary-source verification of the OSICAT endpoint numbers was not obtained.
- LINK-HF (multi-sensor patch), CardioMEMS (CHAMPION, GUIDE-HF) and implant-based HeartLogic were out of scope and not covered here.

## 5. Engagement strategies that kept elderly users engaged for months

### Takeaway
The evidence points to two things: very low task burden (a 30-60 second daily voice recording) and a visible human response loop (nurse or physician contact, as in TIM-HF2, TIM-HF and TEN-HMS). Rigorous comparative data on specific engagement tactics in older HF patients was not found.

### Cited Findings
- COMMUNITY-HF (mean age 69) had 83.5% daily-recording adherence over about 4 years, and more than 90% of respondents reported no difficulty or stress. These are company-linked data — [tctmd](https://www.tctmd.com/news/voice-analysis-picks-patients-risk-hf-hospitalization-community-hf)
- TIM-HF had more than 80% telemonitoring adherence with nurse-guided measurement and centre contact — [Thieme](https://thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/abstract/10.1055/s-0034-1369849)
- The benefit of telemonitoring is greatest in compliant NYHA III-IV patients, and depression compromises it — [Frontiers 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cardiovascular-medicine/articles/10.3389/fcvm.2022.943778/pdf)
- Passive camera edema monitoring is explicitly designed to avoid needing patient adherence — [UCLan BCS abstract](https://clok.uclan.ac.uk/43736)

### Inferences
- Design implications: keep the daily action under 1 minute, use the patient's own baseline (voice), show patients that someone responds, screen for depression, and consider passive sensing for those whose adherence lapses.

### Gaps
- No RCT comparing engagement tactics (reminders, caregiver involvement, gamification, feedback) in older HF telemonitoring was found within the search budget.
